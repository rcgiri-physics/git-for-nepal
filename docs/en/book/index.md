# Teaching Version Control: A Foundation Book for Grades 8–12

*Git for Nepal — an open, offline-first book for students, teachers and anyone starting a project.*

## Preface

Every piece of work has a history. A student's essay grows over a week; a team's website changes every day; a dataset is corrected, extended and re-published; a government policy is revised. **Version control** is the practice — and **Git** the tool — that records that history: what changed, when, who changed it and why, with the power to go back, to work in parallel and to combine everyone's work safely.

Most people learn version control late, in a hurry, from scattered tutorials. This book does the opposite. It builds the idea **slowly and deliberately over five school years**, starting with paper cards in Grade 8 and finishing with a professional team project in Grade 12, so that it becomes a **foundation**: a correct mental model, automatic good habits, and the judgement to use them in any project.

It was written for Nepal — every lesson works in a classroom **with no computer**, with **offline computers**, on a **school network**, or with the **internet** — but nothing in it is specific to one country.

## How every lesson is built

Each module follows the same pattern, so students and teachers always know where they are:

1. **At a glance** — time, materials, delivery tiers, outcomes.
2. **Hook** — a real problem.
3. **Understand it** — the explanation, readable on its own.
4. **See it** — a worked example with real Git output.
5. **Unplugged / concept activity** — paper or visual model.
6. **PRIMM lab** — Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make.
7. **Parsons practice** — put the steps in order.
8. **Common mistakes** — symptom, cause, fix.
9. **Practise more with free resources** — exercises from the best free materials worldwide.
10. **Exit ticket**, **Key points**, **New words**, **Extension quest**, and **Teacher notes** with answer keys.
