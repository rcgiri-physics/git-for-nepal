# Part IX. Books and further reading

A reading list for after this book, grouped by purpose. **Free** items can be read online at no cost; **print** items are commercial books (check your library). Levels: ★ beginner · ★★ intermediate · ★★★ advanced. Links were checked on 11 October 2026; publisher pages that block automatic checks are cited without a link.

## 1. Git itself

| Book | Level | Free? | Why read it | Extends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scott Chacon and Ben Straub, [*Pro Git*](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2), 2nd ed. (Apress, 2014) | ★–★★★ | free (CC BY-NC-SA) | the standard reference; chapters 1–3 for everyone, 7 and 10 for the curious | every grade |
| John Wiegley, [*Git from the Bottom Up*](https://jwiegley.github.io/git-from-the-bottom-up/) | ★★ | free (CC BY) | short; builds Git up from blobs and trees | G12 M1 |
| [*Think Like (a) Git*](https://think-like-a-git.net/) | ★★ | free | "Git is a graph; branches are labels" — the best cure for branch confusion | G9 M3, G11 M5 |
| Raju Gandhi, *Head First Git* (O'Reilly, 2022) | ★ | print | visual, puzzle-filled introduction; good for teachers new to Git | Grades 8–10 |
| Prem Kumar Ponuthorai and Jon Loeliger, *Version Control with Git*, 3rd ed. (O'Reilly, 2022) | ★★–★★★ | print | thorough, careful explanations of every major feature | Grades 11–12 |
| Rick Umali, [*Learn Git in a Month of Lunches*](https://www.manning.com/books/learn-git-in-a-month-of-lunches) (Manning, 2015) | ★ | print | one short lesson a day; good self-study after Grade 9 | Grades 9–10 |
| James Coglan, [*Building Git*](https://shop.jcoglan.com/building-git/) (2019) | ★★★ | print/e-book | you write your own Git in Ruby — the deepest understanding of internals | G12 M1 and beyond |

Also good, in zine form: Julia Evans's illustrated zines about Git (see [Wizard Zines](https://wizardzines.com/comics/)) — short, friendly explanations of confusing parts.

## 2. Working in teams and in open source

| Book | Level | Free? | Why read it | Extends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Karl Fogel, [*Producing Open Source Software*](https://producingoss.com/), 2nd ed. | ★★ | free (CC BY-SA) | how open-source projects are run: governance, communication, releases | Grade 11 |
| VM (Vicky) Brasseur, [*Forge Your Future with Open Source*](https://www.pragprog.com/titles/vbopens/forge-your-future-with-open-source/) (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2018) | ★ | print | a practical guide to making your first contributions | G11 M2–M4 |
| Nadia Eghbal, [*Working in Public: The Making and Maintenance of Open Source Software*](https://press.stripe.com/working-in-public) (Stripe Press, 2020) | ★★ | print | who maintains the software the world runs on, and why it is hard | Grade 11, G12 M9 |
| Eric S. Raymond, [*The Cathedral and the Bazaar*](http://www.catb.org/esr/writings/cathedral-bazaar/) (1999) | ★★ | free essay | the classic essay on open development; read critically — it is a historical view | G11 M7 |
| [Open Source Guides](https://opensource.guide/) (GitHub and contributors) | ★ | free (CC BY) | short guides on contributing, starting and maintaining projects | Grade 11 |

## 3. Software engineering practice

| Book | Level | Free? | Why read it | Extends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Titus Winters, Tom Manshreck and Hyrum Wright, [*Software Engineering at Google*](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book) (O'Reilly, 2020) | ★★–★★★ | free online | chapters on [code review](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch09.html) and [version control and branch management](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch16.html) at enormous scale | G10 M4, G12 M2 |
| David Thomas and Andrew Hunt, *The Pragmatic Programmer*, 20th anniversary ed. (Addison-Wesley, 2019) | ★★ | print | habits of good programmers, including "always use version control" | after school |
| Jez Humble and David Farley, *Continuous Delivery* (Addison-Wesley, 2010) | ★★★ | print | the foundations of CI/CD and releasing safely | G12 M3–M4 |
| Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble and Gene Kim, *Accelerate* (IT Revolution, 2018) | ★★ | print | research evidence on which practices (trunk-based development, CI) help teams | G12 M2–M3 |
| [Trunk Based Development](https://trunkbaseddevelopment.com/) (website) | ★★ | free | everything about the trunk-based strategy | G12 M2 |
| Vincent Driessen, [*A successful Git branching model*](https://nvie.com/posts/a-successful-git-branching-model/) (2010, with a 2020 note) | ★★ | free | the original Gitflow post — read the author's own note at the top | G12 M2 |
| [The Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/) (2001) | ★ | free | one page; the source of "agile" | G11 M7 |

## 6. Suggested reading paths

| You are… | Read in this order |
|---|---|
| a Grade 12 finisher going to university | *Pro Git* ch. 7 → *Think Like (a) Git* → *Software Engineering at Google* ch. 9 and 16 → *The Pragmatic Programmer* |
| a future researcher or data scientist | *A Quick Introduction…* → *Good enough practices…* → *The Turing Way* → *Happy Git…* |
| an open-source enthusiast | Open Source Guides → *Forge Your Future…* → *Producing Open Source Software* → *Working in Public* |
| a teacher new to Git | *Head First Git* or *Pro Git* ch. 1–3 → *A Quick Introduction…* → *Teaching Tech Together* → Part I of this book |
| curious about internals | *Git from the Bottom Up* → *Pro Git* ch. 10 → *Building Git* |
| interested in DevOps | *Accelerate* → *Continuous Delivery* → trunkbaseddevelopment.com |
